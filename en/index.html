Introduction

Hello, my name is Eric Bernier. I am a PhD candidate in public health at the School of Public Health of the Université de Montréal, co-supervised by Dr. Nadia Sourial and Dr. Géraldine Layani.

My project is entitled: Strengthening the intersectoral response provided to the different concurrent social and healthcare needs of older people living at home: profiles, resource mapping and network analysis.

This version describes the poster in four parts: the context, the methods, the expected results, and the impacts and outcomes.

1. Context

older people living at home with concurrent social and healthcare needs that may require an intersectoral response in primary care.

Mobility, food, isolation and housing: one person may have several needs. The presence of services does not establish their mobilisation or an appropriate response.

How is the intersectoral response to concurrent social and healthcare needs organized for different profiles of older people living at home?

Research question and specific objectives

How is the intersectoral response to concurrent social and healthcare needs organized for different profiles of older people living at home?

  1. O1. Identify different profiles of older people living at home with concurrent social and healthcare needs that may require an intersectoral response in primary care.
  2. O2. Map territorial resources that may be mobilized in primary care to address the concurrent social and healthcare needs of different profiles of older people living at home, and characterize territorial variations in their availability, diversity, complementarity and accessibility.
  3. O3. Analyze the organizational configurations of intersectoral networks in primary care mobilized across the territory to address the concurrent social and healthcare needs of different profiles of older people living at home, in order to characterize how they operate.

2. Methods

PROVISIONAL PROTOCOL · sites, partnerships and approvals to be confirmed

Methodological objective 1

Conduct a review of the scientific literature to identify and characterize different profiles of older people living at home with concurrent social and healthcare needs that may require an intersectoral response in primary care, and to determine the needs associated with each of these profiles.

Scoping review · 4 databases

Protocol details
  1. systematic searches, explicit eligibility criteria and two independent reviewers.
  2. populations, primary-care context, social and healthcare needs, definitions, instruments, co-occurrence in the same people and reported profiles.
  3. JBI scoping review, reported with PRISMA-ScR; descriptive synthesis of definitions, associated needs and documented configurations.

Methodological objective 2

Conduct a cartographic and spatial study of resources that can be mobilized in primary care to address the different concurrent social and healthcare needs associated with the profiles of older people living at home, in order to characterize their distribution, availability, diversity, complementarity and territorial accessibility across the different CLSC territories.

6 sectors · ecological analysis

Protocol details
  1. systematic identification of eligible resources in selected sources; explicit inclusion and deduplication rules.
  2. location, sector, relevant needs, documented availability, access conditions, eligibility, opening hours and capacity when available.
  3. cartographic and spatial study in R and QGIS; distribution, availability, diversity, complementarity and territorial accessibility.

Methodological objective 3

Conduct a retrospective social network analysis (SNA) of intersectoral networks mobilized in primary care from CLSC territories to address the concurrent social and healthcare needs of different profiles of older people living at home, in order to characterize their composition, the structure of their relationships, and the nature and intensity of collaboration between actors.

One questionnaire · 12 months · whole and ego networks

Protocol details
  1. one relational questionnaire administered once to organizational actors about the previous 12 months.
  2. ties among all organizations within each territorial boundary; composition, structure and operating arrangements, subject to coverage.
  3. partners of focal organizations or teams, including the CLSC; size, diversity and ego–alter ties. Alter–alter ties require explicit collection.

O1: profiles · O2: resources · O3: networks. Retrospective quantitative observational SNA across several contrasting CLSC territories.

Contact ≠ collaboration ≠ help received ≠ need resolved. The 211 figures and contextual maps are illustrative; they do not replace O3 data collection.
